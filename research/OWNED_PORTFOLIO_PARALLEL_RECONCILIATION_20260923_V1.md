## Unique Parallel B mechanisms admitted

### 1. State-family consistency policy

Different Sol state families should eventually declare their own:
- semantic owner;
- consistency/currentness requirement;
- write policy;
- stale-read policy;
- reconciliation rule;
- recovery fence;
- continuity-bearing status.

Wants, historical evidence, active task state, effect receipts, behavior targets, and scratch state should not share one global persistence/currentness rule.

### 2. Qualification exposure lineage

Evidence used to modify a behavior target, model, policy, or training artifact becomes exposed regression evidence for the successor.

It may still be useful, but it may not be described as untouched independent holdout/generalization evidence for the successor.

### 3. External-provider advertisement

A provider should advertise more than a friendly capability label:
- provider/model/runtime identity and version;
- supported operations;
- evidence class produced;
- authority/effect ceiling;
- replay/idempotency semantics;
- provenance/currentness binding;
- health/availability;
- native/external classification.

Provider discovery is not provider trust or authorization.

### 4. Essential-dependency test

An external service that merely improves capability can remain a replaceable peripheral.

If removing it destroys an essential continuity-bearing function rather than merely degrading capability, it has become part of Sol's effective substrate/dependency boundary and must be governed accordingly.

### 5. Capability escalation ladder

Where risk or real-world effects grow with capability, qualify progressively rather than jumping from conceptual competence to actuation.

A generic ladder is:
`synthetic -> offline/replay -> live read-only -> human-assisted -> supervised effect -> tightly bounded autonomy`.

The exact ladder is domain-specific.

### 6. Abstraction promotion gate

A donor mechanism can be useful without becoming shared infrastructure.

Promoting a mechanism into a general Sol subsystem should require evidence that the abstraction reduces more complexity than it introduces, preserves semantic ownership, has fallback/rollback, and survives hostile review.
